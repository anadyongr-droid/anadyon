## 1. E34 — the root cause, found on the third attempt

**The certificate was never the cause, and my 24 October conclusion is
withdrawn.** On 24 October I closed E6, reported the certificate valid, and
claimed the mechanism was: expired certificate → Gmail's relay to
`mail.anadyon.gr` fails TLS → Gmail falls back to sending as Google → mail
unauthenticated.

That made a testable prediction — fix the certificate and the Yahoo bounce stops.
**It did not stop.** A bounce dated 25/26 October, two days after the reissue,
named sending IP `209.85.208.51` → `mail-ed1-f51.google.com`.

So the simpler reading, consistent with every bounce held: **the alias had been
sending through Google all along**, and the certificate's expiry on 12 October
was a real but separate fault that happened to sit next to this one in the
calendar. I reasoned from a coincidence of dates to a causal chain. The
11 September `relay12.grserver.gr` bounce is withdrawn with it — a bounce issued
*by* a host does not make that host the sender of later mail.

**That is three superseded readings of one incident**, all kept in
`EMAIL-DELIVERABILITY.md`: "always broken, merely tolerated"; "a reputation
slide, E6 not implicated"; "E6's certificate is the root cause". Each pointed the
fix somewhere different. The one that survived contact with a test is the
plainest — **the sending path was wrong**.

### Why it could not have been fixed on the Google side

Ruled out explicitly, because it is the repair that occurs to everyone:
authorising Google in SPF. Two independent reasons. Yahoo's own refusal says
*"SPF alone is not sufficient"* and demands DKIM. And **a consumer Gmail account
cannot DKIM-sign a custom domain** — that is a Workspace feature published at
`google._domainkey`, confirmed absent in live DNS. So both viable fixes had to
move the sending away from Google, which is not a coincidence: the domain's two
working DKIM keys belonged to the two senders that *were* authenticated, Papaki
and Resend. Google was the one path with no key.

## 2. The Zoho migration, executed

Done with Tasos at the panel through the afternoon. Order chosen so the fix
could be **proven before any inbound mail moved**: DKIM and SPF first, test, MX
last.

**Values settled from his console rather than from documentation**, which
mattered twice:

- MX is `mx.zoho.eu` / `mx2` / `mx3` at 10/20/50 — the **EU** data centre, so
  the generic `mx.zoho.com` values in Zoho's help pages were indeed wrong for
  this account.
- The SPF include is **`zohomail.eu`**, not the `zoho.eu` I had suggested — and
  the console's value is the better one, being Mail-specific rather than
  covering every Zoho product.

**The SPF row came through blank**, so the candidates were resolved rather than
guessed: `zoho.eu` → `spf.zoho.eu`; `one.zoho.eu` adds ZeptoMail and zcsend;
`zohomail.com` is US infrastructure. Took Zoho's **merged** option,
`v=spf1 include:zohomail.eu +mx include:_spf.fastmail.gr -all`, over their first
option for two reasons their generic advice cannot know: it keeps `-all` rather
than weakening to `~all` at the exact moment the domain is being spoofed, and it
keeps Papaki and fastmail authorised while nothing has proven they are unused.
**Counted the lookup budget: 8 of SPF's 10**, with `relay.mailchannels.net`
terminating in plain IPs rather than chaining further.

### CONFIRMED by headers, not by silence

```
dkim=pass   header.i=@anadyon.gr  header.s=zmail
spf=pass    136.143.169.15 (sender-op-o15.zoho.eu)
dmarc=pass  header.from=anadyon.gr
DKIM-Signature: s=zmail; d=anadyon.gr; i=customerservice@anadyon.gr
```

Every element of the diagnosis became a reading rather than a reasoning,
including the selector — I named `zmail` as an inference and labelled it as one;
the headers confirm it. Zoho's own ARC seal agrees end to end.

**Inbound confirmed separately**: a test from `anadyon.gr@gmail.com` to
`customerservice@anadyon.gr` arrived in Zoho. **The MX cleanup is complete** —
10/20/50 on Zoho, the Papaki MX deleted, verified propagated.

**E34 is closed.**

### Tasos caught a real hole, and it is the lesson of the day

I wrote that the absence of a bounce on the Zoho→Yahoo send was *conclusive*. He
asked: *"unless we are not receiving emails"*. **He was right.** The message was
sent as `customerservice@anadyon.gr`, whose Return-Path now resolves through the
new MX to Zoho — so if inbound were broken, a bounce could not be delivered and
the silence would mean nothing.

**Same error as the Certificate Transparency reading two days earlier**: treating
an absence as evidence without first establishing that the absence could have
been a presence. Two days apart, identical shape. Caught in minutes this time
only because it was questioned. The inference was not wrong; the order of
operations was — the premise needed testing first, which is what the inbound test
then did.

## 3. E38 — found, then downgraded the same day

Found while answering whether the alias settings are reachable from the Android
app. Google's own page: *"Starting January 2027, Gmail will no longer support the
'Send as' feature for third-party email addresses."* `customerservice@anadyon.gr`
was squarely in scope — a third-party address on a consumer account.

For a few hours that made the Zoho cutover a **deadline** rather than a
preference. By the evening the cutover was done, so **E38 is downgraded to
housekeeping**: Gmail still withdraws the feature, but office mail no longer uses
it. One action remains — remove the alias.

**Two things worth keeping from the scope reading.** The withdrawal covers *"Gmail
on the web **or in the Gmail mobile apps**"*, so the phone is not a way round it —
which an earlier reading of a search summary had suggested it might be. But the
mobile Gmail app **with the mailbox added as a full IMAP account** does survive,
and Google recommend it for sending; that is not *Send as*, it is Gmail acting as
an ordinary client over Zoho's SMTP. Desktop web cannot do this.

**And forwarding is not a workaround**, which Tasos asked about directly: it
carries mail *in* and cannot change what it goes out *as*, because the From
address belongs to the sending account. Forwarding plus plain Gmail gives staff
the mail and the customer the wrong address.

## 4. Papaki's DNS panel, and two records at broken names

**The panel appends the zone to whatever is typed** — its input reads
`[ ____ ] .anadyon.gr` and its help text says to leave the field *blank* for the
apex, not `@`. Zoho's instructions say `@`, which is the common convention and
not Papaki's.

This produced two live records at doubled names, because Zoho's table writes its
ZeptoMail rows fully-qualified while writing the mail row relative:
`bounce-zem.anadyon.gr.anadyon.gr` and
`24165319._domainkey.anadyon.gr.anadyon.gr` — the second a DKIM key that would
have silently never verified. Both deleted; both were ZeptoMail, which this
project does not use because transactional mail goes through **Resend** on
`send.anadyon.gr`.

**The general rule, recorded:** if a panel shows the zone as a suffix beside the
input, type only the part to the left of it.

## 5. Where the DNS move stands, and W64

`DNS-MOVE-TO-CLOUDFLARE.md` written on request, with **every record read from
live DNS today** rather than copied forward — because the whole risk of that
migration is one record quietly not making the trip. Two traps named: Cloudflare's
scan cannot enumerate TXT names it has no reason to try, **and this zone's three
DKIM selectors are exactly that**; and Cloudflare defaults A records to proxied,
which in front of Vercel means a redirect loop and in front of mail means mail
stops.

**Corrected by Tasos:** the runbook said to *create* a Cloudflare account. There
already is one — **the backups are in Cloudflare R2**. So it is adding a zone,
not onboarding a vendor.

**Which opened W64**, the more useful consequence: R2 holds the only off-site copy
of the production database, so moving the zone into that account means one account
lost or compromised takes out both the DNS and the backups — the latter being what
you reach for while fixing the former. Not an argument against moving, since
leaving DNS with an unresponsive provider is a worse and daily risk. But two
things follow: 2FA on that account is now load-bearing for two systems and its
recovery address must **not** be `customerservice@anadyon.gr`; and a second backup
destination is worth more than it was.
