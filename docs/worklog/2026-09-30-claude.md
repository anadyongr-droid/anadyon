## What was decided, and why

- **Famozo over Riderly, on principle rather than difficulty.** Riderly's 403 can only be
  passed by presenting as a browser, and `lib/competitorRates.ts` and
  `lib/podilatadikoRates.ts` both send `AnadyonRatesBot/1.0` under the comment *"Identify
  honestly rather than impersonating a browser."* Faros is not a precedent: it never
  refused a non-browser client, it just rendered in JavaScript. Famozo serves plain HTTP,
  so a scraper there keeps the convention and costs nothing per run. Whether to make an
  exception for a competitor that has declined us is Tasos's call, and it is not needed.
- **The broker's list is recorded as an assertion, not a certificate** (§8). It is dates
  typed into an email — the better source for *what is current*, the weaker for *what is
  covered*, carrying no policy number, insurer or terms. B2 and B5 still need the PDFs.
- **Nothing was fixed inside the audit**, per W19's own terms. Five items, five fixes,
  separately reviewable.

## What the audit found

Five rows built (Supabase auth, Storage, Resend, Stripe, Competitor feeds), two partly
(Supabase data, AADE), two not at all (Wise, SMS). Detail in §5.3a. Three worth naming:

- **`app/api/admin/sms/route.ts` throws.** `await client.messages.create(...)` with no

  the same table.

  silence — and nothing records that a deposit is expected.
- **The universal timeout rule is honoured in four files.** The August outage *was* an
  unbounded call, and the fix went to that call rather than to the rule.

**The reusable part:** competitor-feeds was diagnosed as "a rule in a table nobody
re-reads". True but incomplete — the table had never been read against the code at all, so
nine rows carried the same risk silently. The verdict now lives in the table, with an item
number beside each gap.
