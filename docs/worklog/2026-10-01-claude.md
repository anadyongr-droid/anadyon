## What was decided, and why

  Zakynthos operator publishes motorbike prices over plain HTTP. That was wrong, and the

- **`pickBandDays` refuses rather than guesses.** Their bands are 1–3, 4–6 and 7+ days and

- **No October figure is stored**, because they publish none — low season is May, June and

## What was decided, and why

  derivable from columns that already exist. `deposit_paid_at` has been there since migration

- **Urgency, not age, is the ranking.** A deposit unpaid two days on a booking collecting

  every unreconciled deposit rather than Wise's specifically. A superset, strictly better

## What was decided, and why

- **Browser `fetch("/api/…")` is deliberately out of scope.** A hung same-origin
  request costs one spinner in one tab and the user can reload. An unbounded call
  inside a serverless function holds the invocation open until the platform kills
  it, which is the failure §5.3 names.
- **The four already-bounded callers were left in their own shapes.** A signal
  passed into a closure, a reason string for `alert_outbox`, a `Promise.race`
  around an SDK that takes no signal. Rewriting working code to route through a
  new module is churn with a regression risk and no gain, so the test accepts any
  of those; what it rejects is a bare `fetch`.
- **The timeout does not decide what happens next, on purpose.** §5.3's three
  rules differ per caller: a timed-out scrape is a skipped source, a Telegram
  alert is queued, and an AADE filing is ambiguous. One helper cannot answer
  that, so it throws and each caller keeps its own answer.
- **AADE's ambiguity went onto W26 rather than being invented here.** A filing
  that times out may have been accepted with the answer lost; a 422 definitely
  was not accepted. Both write `dcl_status = "error"`, so a backlog view would
  offer "resubmit" for a filing that may already be lodged — and a duplicate
  statutory submission is worse than a late one. Whether an unknown filing is
  resubmitted automatically or only after a human checks the AADE portal
  **changes what staff must do, so it is Tasos's under §13.** Propose, not merge.

## W27 reviewed — three findings, all real, all mine

The review bot on #190 raised three. I verified each against the code before
touching anything, and all three held. Recorded as classes rather than slips,
because the class is the reusable part.

- **A bounded call is not a bounded response.** `fetch` resolves when *headers*
  arrive and the abort signal stays attached to the body stream, so both AADE
  routes read `.text()` outside their recovery block. A filing that stalled after
  headers would have escaped *after* `claim_dcl_submission` set
  `dcl_status = 'submitting'` — and that claim refuses anything already
  `submitting`, so the filing becomes **permanently unretryable**, recoverable
  only by a manual `UPDATE`. Worse than the bot described; I checked the
  migration to find that out. The shape pre-dated the timeout, but bounding the
  call turned a rare reset into something the budget itself triggers.
- **A per-call budget does not bound a route.** 20s is safe for one call and
  unsafe for a route making several: four EzCar searches with three *mandatory*
  10s crawl delays under `maxDuration = 60` spend half the ceiling sleeping, and
  the final batch adds four more fetches. The invocation would die before
  `writeCursor`, losing the batch — a timeout with no degraded state, which §5.3
  forbids. **This was my own module doc applied per call instead of per route.**
- **A guard that can be fooled is worse than no guard, because it reports
  clean.** The sweep read a 14-line window, so a bare `fetch(url)` immediately
  followed by a bounded call passed — the shape a careless addition actually
  takes. It now parses the balanced call expression.

## What was decided

- **No constant could fix the scrape budget, and that is why `routeBudget`
  exists.** The call count varies from four to eight, so any figure small enough
  for the worst case is needlessly short for the common one and would make a
  slow-but-working source fail. A shared ceiling drawn down is the only shape
  that fits both. It counts the crawl delays, holds a reserve for the cursor
  write, and skips a call too short to be worth starting so the cursor stays
  where work stopped.
- **Balanced-bracket scan, not an AST parse.** The bot asked for an AST. The test
  suite has no TS parser among its dependencies, and adding one to a guard test
  is a dependency to maintain for the life of the rule. The scanner is pinned by
  seven fixtures instead, and I said on the thread that the fixture set is where
  the next mis-read goes — and asked for a case if they can see one.

## What that cost, and the lesson I'd keep

**Writing the fixtures for the scanner found a defect the bot had not raised.**
The balanced expression still contained its own string contents, so
`fetch("https://x/a)b?signal:1")` matched `/\bsignal\s*[:,}]/` and cleared a bare
call — a token that *looks* like the fix but is data. I only found it because I
wrote the fixture expecting it to pass and it failed.

That is the second time today the same thing happened: the test I wrote to check
my work was itself the thing that needed checking. Mutation testing catches a
vacuous assertion; **a fixture catches a scanner that is confidently wrong**, and
those are different instruments. Worth keeping both.

One collateral fix: `lib/rateImportLog.test.ts` matched `if (done) {` literally
and broke on the new condition. Its subject is *which branch records the import*,
not how the branch is spelled, so it now matches the `done` guard — and I
confirmed it still fails when the record is moved out of that branch, rather than
assuming the relaxed version still bites.
