## Got wrong, and how it was caught

**The first redaction attempt failed and its check was worthless.** I painted
white boxes over the front page's fill-in zones, then "verified" by counting ink
*inside those boxes* — which is necessarily zero, because I had just painted them
white. The check could not fail, so it proved nothing. Viewing the output showed
the driver's name, the full licence number and the notes line still legible.

That is the tautological-test failure `AGENTS.md` warns about, made on the one
task where the cost of being wrong is permanent. The second check compared
against the original at a stricter threshold, **did** fail honestly on page 2,
and its two hits turned out to be a paper crease and the page edge — confirmed by
looking rather than by asserting.

The deeper reason redaction was abandoned: the blank form is printed in
red-orange and the customer wrote in red and blue pen, so no colour rule
separates the two. It was not a tuning problem.

## Findings

Nine mismatches, in `CONTRACT-VS-WEBSITE.md`. The three that matter:

- **The website advertises theft cover and CDW as included in all rentals.** The
  contract sells both as paid options with a non-waivable excess, and no
  certificate carries either. New item W11.
- **The contract already permits motorbikes from 18**, while the website says 21
  for everything. The contract is the one aligned with Greek licence law. N5.
- **The contract already requires a licence held one year**, which the website
  never mentions and which Intersalonica Article 19 prices. N4 was asking whether
  to adopt this rule; we already contract on it.

Also: article 16 cites the repealed Law 2472/1997 and bundles marketing consent
into the rental, and article 4(f) cross-references the wrong article for
insurance.
