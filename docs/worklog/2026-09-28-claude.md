## What was decided, and why

- **Jev (TypeSafe AI) declined.** Blueprint §10. It returns typed decisions and no text,
  so it covers two of the five fields `emailClassifier` produces and would sit *in front
  of* the Claude call rather than replace it — a second vendor and a second failure mode
  to save part of a single-digit-euro bill. The blocking reason is data protection, not
  engineering: the classifier processes customer correspondence. Three conditions for
  revisiting are recorded so the question is not researched again.
- **Freshness reports the OLDEST source, not the newest.** The comparison grid draws on
  all four at once, so re-importing EzCar this morning does not make a months-old Faros
  column current.
- **The source registry is hand-maintained rather than derived from the stored rows.**
  Derived from data, a source that has *never run* would not appear — which is the case
  an administrator most needs to see.
- **Ages are counted in calendar days in `Europe/Athens`, not elapsed hours.** See the
  correction below.

## What was corrected — three times, and each is worth reading

1. **"No such model" on Jev.** Tasos meant JEV, not Jet. My first search was for the
   wrong name and I reported, confidently, that nothing existed.
2. **"The insurance contracts are not in the email."** They are — PDF attachments from
   the broker `kexpress2007@gmail.com`. My search required *ασφάλιση / ασφαλιστήριο /
   συμβόλαιο*; every one of the broker's subjects reads **ΑΣΦΑΛΕΙΑ** or **ΑΣΦΑΛΕΙΕΣ**,
   which Gmail does not stem across. **Search the correspondent, not the vocabulary.**
3. **The account of the freshness bug.** I wrote that the age was "computed on the server
   in UTC while the date was formatted in the browser's zone". The age was
   `floor(elapsed_ms / 86400000)` — **pure elapsed time, with no timezone in it at all**.
   Tasos caught it: at UTC+2 against Athens at UTC+3 with midnight far away, the dates
   could not have differed. The client-side formatting is a real but *latent* hazard,
   sized by the offset difference — one hour for a reader at UTC+2, seven for New York.
